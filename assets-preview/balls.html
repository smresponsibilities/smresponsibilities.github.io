Scale test — the actual argument

This is the whole decision. The drawn ball is sharp at 96px, 48px, 24px and 16px. The original sprite is ~30px of pixel art — fine small, mushy when scaled up. You need a ball in the header and inline at 16px next to an employer name, so the SVG wins on practicality before any licensing argument.

Why every ball carries a description

You're right that the icons are meaningless alone — almost nobody reads a Nest Ball and thinks "on-campus." So the ball never appears without its label. At rest it shows NEST BALL · On-campus; hovering or tapping opens the full canon reason. This is the §7.5 legibility rule: a tooltip explains, it never stores. The meaning is on screen before anyone interacts.

Cost

One <symbol>, about 6 lines. Thirteen balls = thirteen two-property CSS rules. Total footprint under 1 KB, zero network requests, and it recolours with the version skins. The original sprites are 13 files at ~280 B each, fixed forever at ~30 px.
